# Voice

How Alex writes, so drafted site copy sounds like him. Written by Claude from his own writing;
Alex corrects it. Every draft in [the copy plan](../plans/active/copy-plan.md) follows this file.

- **Status:** draft, waiting for Alex's corrections
- **Read:** all 14 posts in `archive/writing/` (2025), the IA docs' Primary visitor, Primary
  action and Notes sections, `publishing` (`boring-site/ethics.md` and the three plans), the 10
  Medium posts in the RSS feed (2019–2023), three posts from `blog` (2019), the `alexfinnarn`
  README, project READMEs, and the start of the component library talk deck
- **Not read:** `blurg`, `ds-presentation`, `talks-behat-intro`, older Medium posts

## The short version

- A guy talking to you across a table, not a brand talking to a market.
- Opinions first, then the story from his own work that earned the opinion.
- Long, loose sentences broken up by short lines that land on their own.
- Funny at his own expense more than anyone else's.
- Plain words. Scare quotes around other people's jargon.

## Openings

He almost never opens with a thesis. He opens with one of these:

- **A scene from his day**, usually small and a bit absurd
  - "I was scrolling through my X feed when I saw a gem amongst all the AI news and clickbait."
  - Sidewinder Coffee in Northside, "caffeinated to the gills," on a Sunday, staring at the bunny
  - "As I sit in my hotel room watching some mindless TV…wait a minute, my 'hotel room' seems to
    be my bedroom this year."
- **Something he read and disagrees with**: a LinkedIn post, an X post, an interview, a GitHub
  issue reply. He quotes it, then takes it apart.
- **An everyday analogy** that the rest of the piece leans on: Jenga for complexity, flipping TV
  channels for intent, a 200-tool toolbox for MCP servers.
- **A one-line jolt**: an error message ("Error: Context window limit reached...you suck!!"),
  or a title answered in four words ("When To Move On" / "When you feel like it.").

## Closings

- **A maxim he wants you to remember**
  - "Remember this: when you can't explain your workflow in plain markdown, you know you've
    exceeded minimal viable complexity."
- **A punchline in a list of three**
  - "A web where content is king, intent is queen, and the layout builder is the jester!"
- **An admission**, often aimed at "you youngsters," where experience bit him
  - "It is okay to have an idea, work on it a little bit, and then have experience and wisdom
    bite you in the ass telling you to quickly abandon the idea."
- **A cheerful sign-off**: "IT's gonna get real fun, y'all! Stay tuned...", "Here's to 'vibe
  coding'!"
- **For how-to posts**, a plain bullet list of what to actually do.

## Sentences and rhythm

- Most sentences are medium to long and conversational, strung together with "and," "but,"
  "so." They read like speech.
- Then a short line, often its own paragraph, to land the point:
  - "I don't think so."
  - "Truth is: there is a lot of cost to using open source software."
  - "Don't do it."
- Paragraphs are short: two to four sentences.
- Rhetorical questions come in clusters, usually right before he answers them. "So why choose to
  write so much code?"
- Ellipses do a lot of work: asides, comic timing, a thought trailing off.
  "So my message to all the devs out there…get ready for it…Don't do it."
- He repeats himself on purpose for emphasis: "I've never, ever, ever switched databases."
- He sets up a strawman objection and answers it: "You've probably been waiting to shout this
  phrase…'But The Client Won't Go For That!'"

## Humor

- **Self-deprecation is the main mode.**
  - On organizing conferences: "judging by the number of groups I saw die, I'm not sure I could
    say I'm that good at the organizing part."
  - On scraping LinkedIn: "I'm sure this is fine...says the non-lawyer."
  - The lawn mower that smirked at his crooked window cut, "well, that didn't happen in real
    life, but it did happen in my mind."
- **Made-up words and acronyms**: "scrool" (scrolling while drooling), "CMaRS" (Content
  Management and Rendering System), "obshittian," "Time Burglar."
- **Pop and history references** used as hooks: Gang of Four, John Glenn's "get the girl to check
  the numbers," Game of Thrones, the gorilla experiment, a Pennywise gif.
- **Dad jokes** show up more in the older writing ("I ate some emojis as well").

## Swearing

- Moderate in tech posts: "Hell," "Heck," "dicking around," "bite you in the ass," "slap the
  shit out of myself."
- Heavier in personal posts (the shipping container windows post).
- Used for emphasis or a laugh, never at a reader.

## Words he uses

- Openers and turns: "So," "Heck," "Hell," "Granted," "In fact," "Don't get me wrong," "Sure,
  … but," "Truth is:", "Just think about it;", "Picture this:", "Hence,"
- Ideas he keeps coming back to: first principles, intent, over-engineered, bloated,
  abstractions, workflows, "on the cheap," "the hype," complexity, natural language, owning your
  content, the truth
- Folksy register: "y'all," "getter done," "This ain't my first CMS rodeo," "eat your lunch"
- Scare quotes around words he's skeptical of: "experts," "the news," "non-technical users,"
  "killer feature X"

## Words he wouldn't use

Claude's inference from what's absent and what he mocks. Alex to confirm:

- Marketing and LinkedIn language: leverage, solutions, synergy, passionate, empower, seamless,
  cutting-edge, world-class, "I help businesses…"
- Thought-leader framing: "In today's fast-paced world," "Here's the thing," "Let that sink in"
- Hedged corporate humility: "I'm humbled to…"

## What he pushes back on

From the posts:

- Hype, and the "paid posts that hype models around the time of release"
- Gatekeepers and "experts" who aren't curious
- Abstractions and code that solve problems nobody has
- The "I'm not technical" trope used to sell complex UIs
- "The client won't go for that": fear dressed up as realism
- Sales pitches that bend the truth ("Oh there's no cost, it's open source!")
- Listicle advice ("7 steps, 12 steps, or 4 steps")

From the IA answers, about this site:

- Pretension
- "Not a fit for" lists
- Resumes and bio downloads
- Different bios for different audiences
- Leading with client names instead of the problem
- Proxies and group affiliations, which he dislikes but admits the world runs on

## How he talks about himself

- Direct about strengths, then undercuts it with a joke. He doesn't brag in a straight line.
- Openly says he's an outsider: "I am not someone these prospective clients can relate to. I am
  not normal."
- Admits what he hasn't done yet: "partially since I have not explored those domains yet."
- Calls himself a generalist and a "shapeshifter" who goes to "as many disparate events as an
  industrious tech recruiter would."
- Backs claims with his own history and, when he has them, numbers (the Drupal download sizes,
  the 67% token cut).

## 2019–2023 vs 2025

- **2019–2023** (Medium, `blog`)
  - Warmer and more personal: van life, job hunting, meetup recaps, his parents' weekly calls
  - More rambling, more emoji and dad jokes, more "I'm figuring this out" reflection
  - Community-minded: organizing groups, conference write-ups, helping others contribute
  - Measured opinions
- **2025** (this site)
  - Sharper and more opinionated. Contrarian on purpose
  - Stronger claims, made with more confidence and less hedging
  - More swearing in places
  - Sometimes punches at people rather than ideas: "incredibly dumb," "some dumb developer"
- **For the site, Claude suggests:** the 2025 confidence and point of view, with the older
  warmth and self-deprecation. Sharp about ideas, not about people. Site copy is short, so it
  will mostly use his punchy one-liner register, not the long rambles. Alex to decide.

## Rules for drafts

- Fix typos and grammar. His posts have a lot of them; the drafts shouldn't copy that.
- No fact, number, name or date that isn't in his writing, the IA docs, or his answers.
- No marketing words (see "Words he wouldn't use").
- First person, contractions, plain words.
- One joke at most in a short piece of copy, and it's on him.
